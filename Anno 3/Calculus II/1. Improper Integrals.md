Integrals:
- Necessary condition for existence:
	- $f$ must be bounded.
		- $\exists M > 0 : |f(x)| \leq M \qquad \forall x \in [a,b]$
	- If $f$ is [[5. Funzioni continue e limiti notevoli#^87ef98|continuous]]  $\Rightarrow$
		- $f$ is integrale.
		- There is always a function $F$ called **primitive anti-derivative**.
			- $F'(x) = f(x) \qquad \forall x \in [a,b]$
		- $\int_{a}^{b}f(x)dx = F(b)-F(a)$ ^707598
			- Fundamental theorem of integral calculus.

Improper integrals:
- Reason:
	- To extend the integral use.
- Unbound domain:
	- First kind:
		- Function:
			- $f:[a,+\infty] \quad \Rightarrow \mathbb{R}$
			- $\int_{a}^{\infty}f(x)dx = \lim\limits_{b\rightarrow\infty}F(b)-f(a)$
				- By [[1. Improper Integrals#^707598 |definition]].
		- Cases:
			- **Convergent**:
				- $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}F(x) = l \in \mathbb{R}$
					- $\int_{a}^{+\infty}f(x)dx = l-F(a) < +\infty$
				- Additional **necessary** condition:
					- $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}f(x) = 0$
			- **Divergent**:
				- $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}F(x) = \pm\infty$
					- $\int_{a}^{+\infty}f(x)dx = \pm\infty$
			- **Oscillating**:
				- $\nexists\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}F(x)$
		- General example: 
			- $f(x) = \frac{1}{x^{\rho}}= x^{-\rho}$ with $\rho > 0$
				- $F(x) = \left\{ \begin{array}{ccc} \ln(x) &   & \rho = 1 \\ \frac{x^{-\rho+1}}{-\rho+1} &   & \rho \neq 1 \end{array} \right.$
				- $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty} F(x) = \frac{1}{-\rho+1} \lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty} x^{-\rho+1} =$
					- $0$ if $\rho > 1$ 
						- **Convergent**
					- $\infty$ if $0 < \rho \leq 1$
						- **Divergent**
	- Second kind:
		- Function:
			- $f:]a,b] \quad \Rightarrow \mathbb{R}$
			- $\int_{a}^{b}f(x)dx = F(b) - F(a) = F(b) - \lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a^{+}} F(x)$
				- By definition can be written also as: $\lim\limits_{c\rightarrow a^{+}} \int_{c}^{b}f(x)dx$
		- Cases:
			- **Convergent**:
				- $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a^{+}} F(x) = l \in \mathbb{R}$
					- Only if $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a^{+}} F(x) < +\infty$
			- **Divergent**:
				- $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a^{+}} F(x) = +\infty$
		- General example: 
			- $f(x) = \frac{1}{(x-a)^{\rho}}= (x-a)^{-\rho}$ with $\rho > 0$
				- $F(x) = \left\{ \begin{array}{ccc} \ln(x-a) &   & \rho = 1 \\ \frac{(x-a)^{-\rho+1}}{-\rho+1} &   & \rho \neq 1 \end{array} \right.$
				- $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a^{+}} F(x) = \frac{1}{-\rho+1} \lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty} (x-a)^{-\rho+1} =$
					- $\infty$ if $\rho \geq 1$ 
						- **Divergent**
					- $0$ if $0 < \rho < 1$
						- **Convergent**
- **First criterion of Asymptotic Comparison**:
	- On the first kind:
		- $f,g : [a, +\infty[ \quad \Rightarrow \mathbb{R}$
			- $f(x)\geq0 , g(x)\geq0 \qquad \forall x \in [a,+\infty[$
		- $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}f(x) = \lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}g(x) = 0$ 
			- $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are infinitesimal.
		- $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = l \qquad (l>0)$
			- This means that they tend to $0$ with the same speed:
				- $f = O(g)$
	- On the second kind:
		- $f,g : ]a,b] \quad \Rightarrow \mathbb{R}$
			- $f(x)\geq0 , g(x)\geq0 \qquad \forall x \in  ]a,b]$
		- $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a^{+}}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = l \qquad (l>0)$
			- This means that they tend to $a$ with the same speed:
				- $f = O(g)$
	- When $f = O(g)$: 
		- $f(x)$ is convergent $\Leftrightarrow$ $g(x)$ is convergent.
		- $f(x)$ is divergent $\Leftrightarrow$ $g(x)$ is divergent.
- **Second criterion of Asymptotic Comparison**:
	- On the first kind:
		- $f,g : [a, +\infty[ \quad \Rightarrow \mathbb{R}$
			- $f(x)\geq0 , g(x)\geq0 \qquad \forall x \in [a,+\infty[$
		- $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}f(x) = \lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}g(x) = 0$
			- $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are infinitesimal.
		- $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = 0$
			- This means that $f(x)$ tends to $0$ faster than $g(x)$:
				- $f = o(g)$
	- On the second kind:
		 - $f,g : ]a,b] \quad \Rightarrow \mathbb{R}$
			 - $f(x)\geq0 , g(x)\geq0 \qquad \forall x \in  ]a,b]$
		 - $\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow a^{+}}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = 0$
			 - This means that $f(x)$ tends to $a$ faster than $g(x)$:
				 - $f = o(g)$
	- When $f = o(g)$: 
		- $g(x)$ is convergent $\Rightarrow$ $f(x)$ is convergent.
		- $f(x)$ is divergent $\Rightarrow$ $g(x)$ is divergent.
- **Not absolutely positive function**:
	- $f : [a, +\infty[ \quad \Rightarrow \mathbb{R}$
		-  $f(x) \in \mathbb{R} \qquad \forall x \in [a,+\infty[$
	- We consider $|f(x)|$:
		- If $\int_{a}^{+\infty}|f(x)|dx < +\infty$
			- $|f(x)|$ is convergent.
			- $f(x)$ is *absolutely* convergent $\Rightarrow$ $f(x)$ is convergent.
