	- Estimate.
		- Developing an estimate of monetary resources needed to complete the work of the project.
		- Methods:
			- Analogous estimate (based on past experiences).
				- Quick but lacks detail.
			- Parametric.
				- Quick and accurate.
			- Bottom-up (individual items estimation are summed for a total cost).
				- Most accurate but slower.
			- Computerized tools.
				- Accurate but the tools cost and time increase.
			- Three Point Estimating (PERT or Non-weighted TPE).
				- Minimises Bias but can be gamed.

- Future and Present value:
	- Data:
		- FV = Future Value 
		- PV = Present Value 
		- r = Interest Rate 
		- n = Number of Periods
	- Future value :
		- $FV = PV * (1+r)^n$
	- Present value:
		- $PV = \frac{FV}{(1+r)^n}$
	- Expected Present value (EPV):
		- Present value analysis that takes into consideration the risk of the opportunity being considered.
		- $EPV = p(PV) * PV$
- Cash Flow analysis:
	- Payback period (PBP):
		- The length of time it takes the company to recoup the initial costs of production.
		- This method compares the initial investment to the cash inflows expected over the life of the product/service.
		- Least precise of all the cash flow calculations.
	- Discounted cash flows:
		- Technique that compares the value of the future cash flows of the project to today’s currency.
		- Higher PV means better investment.
	- Net present value (NPV):
		- Present value is calculated on each year and initial investment is subtracted.
	- Internal rate of return (IRR):
		- The discount rate when the present value of the cash inflows equals the original investment: the value of i so that NPV = 0.
		- Higher IRR means better investment.
- Type of costs:
	- Direct cost:
		- Costs attributed to the project and spent it on the work.
	- Indirect cost:
		- Costs that are not directly corelated to the specific project.
		- These costs could be used by other project too.
	- Fixed cost:
		- Costs that are consistent in the project regardless of how many times is used (one time payments for example).
	- Variable cost:
		- Costs that fluctuate with the work done.
- Direct personnel costs (Person-months):
	- Metric used to express the amount of time devoted to a project.
	- To calculate person-months, you multiply the percentage of effort associated with the project by the number of months of the appointment.
- Cost Analysis:
	- Variables Earned Values (EV):
		- The budgeted value of the work physically performed at a point in time, REGARDLESS of when it was scheduled.
	- Actual Cost (AC):
		- The total money charged to the project to date.
	- Planned Value (PV):
		- The budgeted value of the work that SHOULD have been performed to date.
	- Budget at Completion (BAC):
		- The baseline budget.
	- Cost Variance (CV):
		- $CV = EV-AC$
		- $CV > 0$ is good.
		- $CV < 0$ is bad.
	- Cost Performance Index (CPI):
		- $CPI = EV/AC$
		- $CPI > 1$ is good.
		- $CPI < 1$ is bad.
	- Schedule Variance (SV):
		- $SV = EV - PV$
		- $SV > 0$ is good.
		- $SV < 0$ is bad.
	- Schedule Performance Index (SPI):
		- $SPI = EV/PV$
		- $SPI > 1$ is good.
		- $SPI < 1$ is bad.
	- Estimate at Completion (EAC):
		- The forecasted cost of the project, as the project progresses.
		- $EAC = BAC/CPI$
	- Estimate to Complete (ETC):
		- The amount of money required to complete the remaining work from a given date.
		- $ETC = EAC – AC$
	- Variance at Completion (VAC):
		- Projection of the budget surplus or deficit.
		- $VAC = BAC – EAC$
	- To Complete Performance Index (TCPI):
		- The target CPI that is needed to complete the project at the target budget.
		- $TCPI = (BAC – EV) / (BAC – AC)$
		- $TCPI < 1$ the total cost will be lower than budget.
