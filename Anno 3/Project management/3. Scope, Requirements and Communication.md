Scope management:
- Ensures the project includes **All** and **Only** the work required to complete the project successfully.
- Product scope:
	- The features and functionality that are required of the unique product, service, or result.
- Project Scope:
	- The work performed to deliver features and functionality required by the unique product, service or result.

- Work Breakdown Structure (WBS):
	- Describes the breakdown of work and restricts its contents to listing only project work.
		- Generally created by the project manager and team doing the work.
	- One of the most important pieces of the project management planning process.
		- Work not in the WBS is outside the scope of the project.
		- Once the WBS is complete, a number of key items can start such as network diagram, the schedule, the budget, and risk planning.
	- Various breakdown structures:
		- Organizational Breakdown Structure (OBS):
			- It shows how the project organization is structured to accomplish project activities.
		- Risk Breakdown Structure (RBS):
			- It shows the risks that can potentially occur on a project, broken down by risk category.
		- Resource Breakdown Structure (RBS).
		- Bill of Materials (BOM):
			- It shows components, sub- assemblies, and assemblies used to build a product.
	- ![[Pasted image 20250522220705.png]]
		- Code of accounts is the numbering used in the WBS.
- Collect requirements:
	- Determining, documenting and managing Stakeholders needs and Requirements.
- Focus on Collaboration between Project Manager, Stakeholders, and Business Analysis Professionals to: 
	- Determine problems and needs.
	- Identify and Recommend solutions.
	- Facilitate successful implementation.
	- Elicit, Document, and Manage Stakeholder Requirements.

Requirements management:
 - Helps the Project Manager and team analyse, document, and manage the project requirements. The plan may include direction for:
	 - Planning, tracking, and reporting requirement activities.
	 - Configuration management activities:
	 - Initiate changes to the product, service, or result. 
	 - Analyse the impact of the changes.
	 - Change approval authorization. 
	 - Prioritizing requirements.
	 - Determining product metrics.
	 - Completing the traceability matrix.
		 - Associates requirements with their origin and traces them throughout the project life cycle.

- Management by Objectives (MBO):
	- Determine where a project fits in the “big picture” strategic plan of the organization.
- Basics of prioritizing:
	- Need to select what to implement:
		- Customers (usually) ask for way too much.
		- Balance time-to-market with amount of functionality.
		- Decide which features go into the next release.
	- For each requirement/feature, ask: 
		- How important is this to the customer? 
		- How much will it cost to implement? 
		- How risky will it be to attempt to build it?

Communication Management:
- The processes that ensure that the project and stakeholder information needs are met through development of information artifacts that are shared with the stakeholders.
- Plan Communication Management:
	- A plan relevant to the organization, that meets communication needs, taking in to consideration:
		- Stakeholder information needs.
		- Organization Process Assets.
		- Needs of the project.
	- ![[Pasted image 20250523205727.png]]
	- Monitor communications:
		- Ensuring information needs of the project are met and that the Communications Plan is being followed.
	- Manage Communications:
		- Ensuring collection, creation, storage, retrieval, management, monitoring and disposition of project information.
- Communication:
	- Exchange of information (either voluntary or involuntary).
	- Forms:
		- Ideas.
		- Instructions.
		- Emotions.
	- Ways (formal or informal):
		- Written.
		- Spoken.
		- Gestures.
		- Media.
		- Verbiage.
	- Types:
		- ![[Pasted image 20250523205135.png]]
	- Avoiding miscommunication and misunderstandings through:
		- 5C's
			- Correct grammar and spelling.
			- Concise expressions.
			- Clear purpose.
			- Coherent logical flow of ideas.
			- Controlling the flow of the words and ideas.
		- Additionally:
			- Active listening.
			- Cultural awareness.
			- Enhancing team member skills by using:
				- Persuasion.
				- Motivation.
				- Coaching.
				- Negotiation.
				- Conflict resolution.
		- For cross-cultural:
			- Current emotional state.
			- Cultural influences:
				- Generation.
				- National.
				- Origin.
				- Professional.
				- discipline.
				- Gender.
				- Personality biases.
	- Methods used to share information:
		- Interactive:
			- An exchange of information between multiple parties and fosters a common understanding between participants.
		- Push:
			- Distributes information but does not ensure that the information was received or understood.
		- Pull:
			- Provides information in a centrally accessible location such as the internet.

- Meetings:
	- Meeting rules:
		- When a meeting is scheduled, an agenda should be created and distributed before the meeting to allow people to review and prepare for the meeting. 
		- People should review the agenda and prepare for the meeting.
		- A Meeting Chair should be established and that person should lead the meeting and follow the agenda. 
			- The Chair does not always need to be the Project Manager.
		- Anything added to the meeting outside of the agenda should be added at the end or to a future meeting.
- Methods of communication:
	- Formal meeting:
		- Pros:
			- Facilitates dialog.
			- Facilitates decision making.
			- Facilitates member focus.
		- Cons:
			- More difficult scheduling.
			- Possibly more costly.
			- Difficult virtual teams.
	- Informal meeting:
		- Pros:
			- No scheduling needed.
			- Quick response.
		- Cons:
			- Limited audience and timeframe.
	  - Conference call:
		- Pros:
			- Facilitates scheduling.
		- Cons:
			- Attention not guaranteed.
			- Possibly equipment problem.
	- Web conference:
		- Pros:
			- As a conference call with enabled presentations.
			- Facilitates virtual teams.
		- Cons:
			- Same as conference call.
	- Video conference:
		- Pros:
			- As web conference with video communication.
				- Body communication.
				- Participants accountability.
		- Cons:
			- Possible technical issues.
			- Personal visual preparation needed.
	- Status report:
		- Pros:
			- Facilitates reaching a larger audience.
		- Cons:
			- Push/Pull communication.
				- No interaction.
				- No assurance that it was read or understood.
	- E-mail:
		- Pros:
			- Fast and easy.
			- Enables interactive communication.
			- Facilitates rapport building.
		- Cons:
			- Same as Status report.
			- Possible that not all recipients are on list.
			- Can be misinterpreted.
	- Voice mail:
		- Pros:
			- Fast and easy.
			- Acceptable for factual/non-controversial information.
		- Cons:
			- Limited recording time.
			- No written record.
			- Possible misunderstandings.
	- Webinar:
		- Pros:
			- Facilitates reaching a larger audience.
			- Enables interactive communication.
		- Cons:
			- Possible technical issues.
	- Blog:
		- Pros:
			- Facilitates reaching a larger audience.
			- Can be fun.
			- Can be interactive.
		- Cons:
			- Pull type of communication.
			- Delayed communication.
	- Social media:
		- Pros:
			- Facilitates collaboration.
			- Engages Millennials.
			- Facilitates reaching a larger audience.
		- Cons:
			- Possible disengage from other generations.
	- Extranet or shared folders:
		- Pros:
			- Real time updates.
			- Facilitates collaboration.
		- Cons:
			- Steeper learning curve.
	- Newsletter:
		- Pros:
			- Facilitates reaching a larger audience.
		- Cons:
			- Push type of communication.
			- No interactivity.
- Performance reports:
	- Types:
		- Forecast Reports:
			- Provide information on what is expected to be happening on the project. 
			- Forecast reporting associated with cost can include:
				- Estimate at Completion (EAC).
				- Estimate to Complete (ETC).
				- Variance at Completion (VAC).
		- Progress Reports:
			- Provide information on what has been done recently on the project
		- Status Reports: 
			- Provide information on the present overall state of the project. 
		- Variance Reports: 
			- Show the difference between what was planned to happen and what actually happened. 
		- Earned Value Reports: 
			- Show the state of the schedule, budget, and scope of the project at various points in time.
