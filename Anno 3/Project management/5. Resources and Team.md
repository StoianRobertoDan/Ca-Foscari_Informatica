	- Not order resources that meet the quality standards of the project.
	- Not maintaining correct inventory levels.

Team management:
- Human resource plan:
	- Part of the Project Management Plan
	- Outlines methods for defining, staffing, managing, controlling, and releasing human resources.
	- Should include:
		- Documentation of roles and responsibilities.
		- Project organization charts.
		- Staffing Management Plan.
- Responsibility Assignment Matrix (RAM) chart:
	- This tool lets the project team know who is involved in each area and what they are responsible for in that area.
	- RACI chart:
		- Chart of task and roles, in each intersection there is the level of involvement:
			- Responsibility.
			- Accountable.
			- Consulted.
			- Informed.
- Organizational Breakdown Structure (OBS):
	- Shows a reporting relationship between the resources in an organization.
	- Structure would typically be shown in company divisions, departments and groups.
	- Would not show details related to project organization and work.

	- Theory X and Y:
		- Theory X (authoritarian/pessimistic):
			- This style of management assumes that workers:
				- Dislike their work.
				- Avoid responsibility and need constant direction.
				- Have to be controlled, forced and threatened to deliver work.
				- Need to be supervised at every step.
				- Have no incentive to work or ambition.
			- This approach is very "hands-on" and usually involves micromanaging people's work to ensure that it gets done properly.
			- Authority is rarely delegated, and control remains firmly centralized.
		- Theory Y (participative/optimistic):
			- This style of management assumes that workers:
				- Happy to work on their own initiative.
				- More involved in decision making.
				- Self-motivated to complete their tasks.
				- Enjoy taking ownership of their work.
				- Seek and accept responsibility, and need little direction.
				- View work as fulfilling and challenging.
				- Solve problems creatively and imaginatively.
			- Managers who use this approach trust their people to take ownership of their work and do it effectively by themselves.

	- FSNPA:
		1. **Forming:** Team members first meet, learn their roles and responsibilities, and are cautious with their behaviour as they try to assimilate with the group.
		2. **Storming:** Tensions rise as team members start to speak their minds and solidify their places within the group.
		3. **Norming:** Conflicts start to resolve, team members appreciate each other’s strengths, and respect for authority grows.
		4. **Performing:** The group functions together as a cohesive unit and without as much direction from the team leader.
		5. **Adjourning:** The original goals of the team have been completed, and everyone can move on to new projects.

- Power and power types:
	- Formal:
		- Legitimate power.
	- Reward:
		- Benefits for doing what is needed.
	- Penalty:
		- Penalized for not doing what is needed.
		- Worst form of power.
	- Expert:
		- Earned power through expertise.
	- Referent:
		- Derived from an attitude or presence.
	- Attitude power:
		- Utilizing a middle person for negotiation to minimize personal or attitude effect.
	- Commitment power:
		- Commitment via alliances and partnerships.
	- Competition power:
		- Maximising involvement through competition.
	- Knowledge of needs:
		- Solution focused through knowledge of that is needed.
	- Moral or ethical power:
		- Using moral values in the negotiation process.
	- Persistence power:
		- Sticking to the target after initial rejection.
	- Persuasion power:
		- Focusing on comparison and evidence that relate to the parties experience.
	- Planning power:
		- Preparation followed by negotiation.
	- Precedent power:
		- Utilizing something that has work in the past.
	- Professionalism power:
		- Being professional and practical.
	- Risk power:
		- Utilising calculated risks.
- Conflict:
	- Conflict levels:
		1. Problem to solve:
			- Characteristic:
				- Informal sharing and collaboration.
			- Language:
				- Open and fact-based.
			- Environment:
				- Different opinions or misunderstandings.
		2. Disagreement:
			- Characteristic:
				- Personal protection.
			- Language:
				- Guarded and open to interpretation.
			- Environment:
				- Self protect is prioritised and distancing from the debate.
		3. Contest:
			- Characteristic:
				- Winning.
			- Language:
				- Includes personal attacks.
			- Environment:
				- People take side, finger pointing.
		4. Crusade:
			- Characteristic:
				- Focus on protecting the group.
			- Language:
				- Ideological/dogmatic.
			- Environment:
				- "One side will not change and needs to be removed".
		5. World War:
			- Characteristic:
				- Focus on destroying the other side.
			- Language:
				- Minimal.
			- Environment:
				- No possible constructive outcome.
	- Resolution types:
		- Problem solving (confrontational):
			- Attempts are made to solve the actual problem.
			- Best solution.
		- Compromising:
			- Get everyone involve to find a common ground.
		- Forcing:
			- Direct order to solve something.
			- Worst solution.
		- Accommodating:
			- Focus on the positive and distract from the negative.
		- Withdraw/avoiding:
			- Ignoring the problem hoping it will solves itself or disappears.
			- Not an actual solution.
		- Collaborating:
			- Considering insight and views from variety of sources.
