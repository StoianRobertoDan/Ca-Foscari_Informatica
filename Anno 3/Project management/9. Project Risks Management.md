Project risk:
- Risk vs Uncertainty:
	- Risk: 
		- Number of possible outcomes and the probability of each outcome is known.
	- Uncertainty:
		- Number of possible outcomes but the probability of each outcome is not known.
- Processes:
	- Planning for risk management:
		- The organizations process for risk management activities
	- Identifying potential risk events:
		- Identifying project risk events and sources of overall project risk and documenting their characteristics.
	- Analysis of potential risk events:
		- Qualitative Risk Analysis:
			- Prioritizing potential risk events for further analysis by subjectively assessing their potential probability and impact.
		- Quantitative Risk Analysis:
			- Performing rigorous numerical analysis of identified risk events and other sources of uncertainty to determine the impact on project success.
	- Creating Risk Response Plans:
		- Developing options.
		- Selecting strategies.
		- Agreeing on specific actions to address potential risk events.
	- Implementing Risk Response Plans:
		- Implementing planned risk response if the risk event occurs.
	- Monitoring Risk Response Plan implementation:
		- Monitoring the implementation of a risk response plan.
		- Tracking identified risks.
		- Identifying and analysing newly identified risks.
		- Evaluating risk process effectiveness throughout the project life.
- The objectives of Project Risk Management are:
	- Exploit potential positive risk impacts.
	- Minimize potential negative risk impacts.
- Risk Assessment:
	- Risk identification:
		- List of risk items which threat in a specific context.
	- Risk analysis: 
		- Transform raw risks into decision enabling knowledge.
		- Severity of the identified risks.
	- Risk prioritization: 
		- Identified and analysed risks are ranked by relevant weight.
- Risk Control:
	- Risk Management Plan:
		- Strategize for Risk Response Plans 
		- Risk analysis methodologies 
		- Roles and responsibilities for risk assessment and risk plan implementation 
		- Risk timing 
		- Risk category analysis
		- Which risks are acceptable.
		- Contingency plan.
	- Risk resource repository:

- Risk Response Strategies:
	- Positive:
		- Share:
			- Share responsibility and benefits with a third party to maximize opportunity.
		- Exploit:
			- Ensure the risk and success of event or project.
		- Enhance:
			- Improve the size and capacity of the risk.
	- Negative:
		- Avoid:
			- Plan the risk so it doesn't need to be dealt with.
		- Transfer:
			- Transferring the risk exposure to another party.
		- Mitigate:
			- Minimizes bad characteristics of the risk.
	- Acceptance:
		- Tolerating the risk and dealing with it.
	- Contingent Response Strategy:
		- Could be created if conditions change on the project or to accommodate for worst case scenario.
- Risk Identifiers:
	- Product Size:
		- Estimated size of product in number of programs, files, transactions.
		- Amount of reused software.
		- Number of projected changes to the requirements.
		- Number of users of the product.
	- Business Impact:
		- Effect of this product on company revenue.
		- Visibility of this product by senior management.
		- Number of customers who will use this product.
		- Governmental constraints.
		- Costs associated with late delivery.
	- Customer characteristics:
		- Does the customer have a solid idea of requirements.
		- Is the customer willing to participate in reviews.
	- Process definition:
		- Established common process framework and followed by the team.
		- Management support.
		- Document format.
	- Technology to be built:
		- New technology.
		- Application interface with new software.
		- Performance constraints.
		- New or untested hardware involved.
	- Staff size and experience:
		- Staff have the right skills.
		- Staff received necessary training.
		- Enough people available.
		- Staff committed for entire duration.
- Boehm's Top 10 risks:
	- Personnel shortfalls.
	- Unrealistic schedules and budgets.
	- Developing the wrong features.
	- Developing the wrong user interface.
	- Gold-plating.
	- Continuing stream of requirements changes.
	- Shortfalls in external components.
	- Shortfalls in external tasks.
	- Real-time performance shortfalls.
	- Straining computer science capabilities.
- Pure risk vs Business risk:
	- Pure risk:
		- Risk for which insurance can be purchased, thereby transferring the risk for financial benefit to the party accepting the risk.
	- Business risk:
		- Risk for which insurance cannot be purchased, inherent to the business.
