Projects:
- A project is a series of planned actions.
- Properties of projects:
	- They usually have a Start Date and possibly a End Date.
	- They can be completed when its objectives are accomplished.
	- They should account to the satisfaction of the stakeholders.
	- They can end before its accomplishment due to when the objectives cannot be met or the result is no longer needed.
	- Projects are unique.
	- They are carried out through progressive elaboration.
	- ![[Pasted image 20250219134322.png]]
- Stakeholders:
	- A stakeholder is an individual or a group of individuals with an interest, often financial, in the success of a business. 
	- The satisfaction of the main stakeholders is the main objective of a project (in favor of customer satisfaction).
	- Identifying the key stakeholders should be an early priority for the reason above.
	- The primary stakeholders in a corporation include its investors, employees, customers, and suppliers.
- Knowledge Transfer:
	- It is optimal to include members of the operational area in the project if the project will be incorporated into their future work processes to ease the process of introducing the knowledge of the project to the operational area.
	- They can also assist the project team in defining requirements, developing scope, creating estimates, and so on.
- Project sponsor:
	- It is usually an executive in the organization (also a stakeholder) with the authority to assign resources and enforce decisions regarding the project, gives the authority to start the project and can make tie/breaking decisions.

Organizational Structures:
- Organizations have their own styles, cultures, and ways of communicating that influence how project work is performed.
- Aggressive vs Assertive:
	- Companies with aggressive cultures that are comfortable in a leading-edge position within their industries are highly likely to take on risky projects.
	- Companies with risk-averse culture and that prefer the follow-the-leader position within their industries are highly unlikely to take on risky actions.
- Type of organizations cultures:
	- Functional:
		- These culture is centered on specialties and grouped by function.
			- The organization is divided in specialized departments each with its own set of people with the right skill-set and experience in specific functions.
			- Supervisors and managers are experienced in the area they supervise and are able to give more precise directions and training.
		- Hierarchical structure:
			- ![[Pasted image 20250326184817.png]]
			- There is a chain of command that needs to be followed.
			- The upward career path is more clear and straight. 
		- Each group is managed independently and has a limited span of control.
		- Downside:
			- Project managers have little to no formal authority.
			- The vice president or senior department manager usually have the formal authority.
			- Competition over resources and priorities when multiple projects are undertaken.
		- Project team members are likely to be loyal to their functional manager.
			- The functional managers are responsible for their performance reviews.
			- Their career opportunities lie within the functional department, not within the project team.
	- Project-oriented:
		- The project oriented organization is the opposite of the functional:
			- The focus is on the project, not the structure.
			- Loyalty is developed over the project not the functional manager.
			- Project members report to the project manager, not functional or department manager.
			- ![[Pasted image 20250501224025.png]]
		- Resources are dedicated to the projects and project work.
		- Project managers have the ultimate authority (making decisions, acquiring and assigning resources) and reports directly to the CEO.
		- Teams are formed and often co-located (team members physically works in the same place).
	- Matrix:
		- Takes the advantages of functional and a project oriented organizations.
		- Employees report to a functional manager and at least one project manager.
		- Roles:
			- Functional managers take care of the administrative portion, assigns resources and employees to projects and monitors their work.
			- Project managers are responsible for executing the project and assigning the work based on project activities.
		- The project team is free to focus on the project objectives with minimal distraction from the functional department.
