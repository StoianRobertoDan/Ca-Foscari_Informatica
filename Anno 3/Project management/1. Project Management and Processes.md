Project Management  and Project management Office (PMO):
- The PMO is usually a centralized organizational unit that oversees the management of projects and programs across the organization. ![[Pasted image 20250219170245.png]]
- The PMO is responsible for managing the goals of a set of projects, managing resources across the projects, and managing the inter-dependencies of all the projects.
- PMO supports project managers:
	- Providing a precise project management methodology, including templates, forms, and standards.
	- Mentoring, coaching, and training project managers.
	- Facilitating communication within and across projects.
	- Managing resources.
- It also:
	- Archives project documentation for future reference.
	- Compares project goals with project progress and gives feedback to teams and management.
	- Measures the performance of active projects and suggests corrective actions.
- Project managers:
	- They need to be generalist with problem solving skills and other soft skills like communication, decision making, leadership, ... 
	- Technical skills are not a prerequisite for project manager but it should rely on few technical experts in the team.
		- Experience in the application area you’re working in will always help a bit more.
	- They need to know a bit about every aspect of management (from accounting to strategic planning, supervision, personnel administration, ...).
		- It is important to know how to use the right tools for the project, plan appropriately, and manage the schedule, budget, resources, and risks.
- Important project management skills:
	- Technical project management:
		- Applying project management knowledge to deliver the objectives of the projects.
		- Defining the critical success factors of the project.
		- Developing a project schedule, and knowing when to ask for help.
		- Using the right tools for the project, planning appropriately, and managing the schedule, budget, resources, and risks.
	- Strategic business management:
		- Knowing how the project aligns and relates to the business and business functions such as finance, marketing, customer service, and operations.
		- Knowing the risks and issues involved in the results of the project, the financial impacts and their effects, how business value is maximized, and how to manage the scope and schedule.
	- Communication:
		- Written and oral communications are the backbone of all successful projects.
		- Project documents, meeting updates, status reports; PM has to ensure that the information is explicit, clear, and complete.
		- Once the information has been distributed, it is the responsibility of the people receiving the information to make sure they understand it.
	- Organization:
		- Project documentation, requirements information, memos, project reports, personnel records, vendor quotes, contracts, and much more to track quickly.
		- You will also have to organize meetings, put together teams, and perhaps manage and organize media-release schedules, depending on your project.
		- Time management skills are closely related to organizational skills.
	- Conflict management:
		- It involves problem solving, analyzes of the causes and decision on the course of action.
	- Negotiation and influencing:
		- They are part of the problem solving skills.
		- Influencing is convincing other people to do what is the best choice and requires understanding of of the structures of all the organizations in the project.
		- Power is the ability to get people to do things they wouldn’t do otherwise.
	- Leadership (leaders vs managers):
		- Leaders impart vision, gain consensus for strategic goals, establish direction, and inspire and motivate others to achieve project’s goals.
		- Managers focus on results and are concerned with getting the job done according to the requirements.
		- Project managers must exhibit both characteristics during different times on the project.
	- Team-Building and Motivating:
		- Teams can vary from different part of the organizations and not only.
		- For a good team work and good work result in general, the project manager needs to help the team bond and work together and motivate them.
		- This skills rely on negotiation and influencing skills.

Project Processes:
- Project documents:
	- Project charter:
		- (Project definition or project statement).
		- Base information of the project as:
			- Participants, roles and responsibilities.
			- Objective, key goals and scope.
			- Time and cost goals.
			- Stakeholders and management authority.
			- Issued by Sponsor.
			- Gives PM Authority.
	- Project management plan:
		- A document that describes:
			- Delivery of the benefits, their target and owner.
			- Metrics.
			- Assumptions.
			- Risks.
			- Success Measures.
		- Written after the project charter has been approved.
- The five processes:
	- Initiating process:
		- At the beginning of the project and each project phase.
		- Project charter is set up now.
		- This process authorizes the project manager to start working on the project.
	- Planning process:
		- Formulating and revising project goals and objectives and creating the project management plan.
		- Planning process group also involves determining alternative courses of action and selecting from among the best.
		- This process group is where the project requirements are fleshed out. 
		- Planning has more processes than any of the other project management process groups.
		- Project managers will perform frequent iterations of the Planning processes prior to project completion.
		- Planning must encompass all areas of project management and consider: 
			- budgets, activity definition, scope planning, schedule development, risk identification, staff acquisition, procurement planning, and more.
	- Executing process:
		- It involves putting the project management plan into action.
		- The Executing process group will utilize the most project time and resources, and as a result, costs are usually highest during the Executing processes.
	- Monitoring and Controlling process:
		- It is where project performance measurements are taken and analysed to determine whether the project is staying true to the project management plan.
		- The idea is to identify problems as soon as possible and apply corrective action to control the work of the project and ensure successful outcomes.
	- Closing process:
		- However, Closing is important because all the project information is gathered and stored for future reference.
		- The documentation collected during the Closing process group can be reviewed and used to avert potential problems on future projects.

- Tailoring:
	- The project manager and team are responsible for choosing which processes within each process group are appropriate for the project on which you’re working.
	- Not all projects require all the processes.
- The Process Flow:
	- The five processes are not discrete and singular but interact and overlap with each-other.
	- They are iterative and are prone to re-visitation and revise throughout the project's life.

- Project phases:
	- A project phase generally consists of segments of work that allow for easier management, planning, and control of the work and produce at least one deliverable by the end of the phase.
		- A deliverable is an output that must be produced, verified, and approved to bring the phase, life-cycle process, or project to completion.
		- Deliverables might also include things such as design documents, project budgets, blueprints, project schedules, prototypes, and so on.
	- Phase Attributes:
		- Name
		- Number
		- Duration
		- Resource
		- Requirements
		- Entrance Criteria
		- Exit Criteria
	- Phases in a multiple phase project are typically sequential but there are instances when overlapping or concurrent (iterative) phases are more beneficial. 
		- Waterfall
		- Iterative
		- Progressive Elaboration
