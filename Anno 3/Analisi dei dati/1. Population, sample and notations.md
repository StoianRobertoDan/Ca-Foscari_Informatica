**Glossary**:
 - **Population**: all units of interest.
 - **Parameter**: numerical characteristic of the population of interest. Notation: $\theta$.
 - **Census** (*censimento*): observation of the whole population to value $\theta$.
 - **Sample**: subset/subgroup of the population used to evaluate/estimate $\theta$ when the census is not possible.
 - **Statistic**: any function of the data (either by census or sample).
 - **Estimator**: statistic used to reconstruct $\theta$. Notation: $\widehat\theta$.
 - **Estimate** (*stima*): value of the estimator corresponding to the studied sample.

**Errors**:
 - The difference between the estimate (value of $\widehat{\theta}$) and the parameter ($\theta$) is due to two types of errors:
	- **Sampling** **errors**:
		- Inevitable.
		- Due to the limit of the sample.
		- Decreases more and more as the sample is bigger and if the estimator $\widehat\theta$ is reasonable.
	- **Non**-**sampling** **errors**:
		- Evitabile.
		- Due to the use of inappropriate sampling scheme, wrong statistical techniques or the use of an estimator $\widehat\theta$ that's not appropriated.
		- May or may not change with a bigger sample.

**Random sampling**:
 - **Simple random sampling** is a sampling design where units are collected from the entire population independently of each other, all being equally likely to be sampled.
 - It is a possible way to reduce non-sampling errors.
 - Random sampling reduces distortions and having a specific subgroup of the population.
 - Observations collected by means of a simple random sampling design are **IID** (independent and identically distributed).

**Bias and Consistency**:
 - **Bias** (distorsione): ^498016
	- Is defined as $Bias(\hat\theta) = \mathbb{E}(\hat\theta - \theta) = \mathbb{E}(\hat\theta) - \theta$ 
		- This equality is due to the fact that since $\theta$ is a constant, $\mathbb{E}(\theta) = \theta$ .
	- An estimator $\hat\theta$ is unbiased for a parameter $\theta$ if its expectation equals the parameter: $\mathbb{E}(\hat\theta) = \theta$ for all possible values of $\theta$.
	- Unbiasness means that collecting a large number of samples and computing them on $\hat\theta$ on the average we hit the unknown parameter $\theta$ exactly.
		- Unbiased estimator neither underestimate nor overestimate the parameter on the long run.
 - **Consistency**: ^4b93d5
	- An estimator $\hat\theta$ is consistent for a parameter $\theta$ if the probability of its sampling error converges to 0 as the sample size increases to infinity.
		- $\mathbb{P}\{|\hat\theta - \theta|>\epsilon\} \rightarrow 0 \;\mbox{as}\; n\rightarrow\infty \; \forall\; \epsilon > 0$
		- $|\widehat{\theta}-\theta|$ is called estimation error. 
	- The consistency doesn't require $\widehat{\theta}$ to be unbiased:
		- It's required to be asymptotically unbiased: ^cb8b47
			- $Bias(\widehat{\theta}) = \mathbb{E}(\widehat{\theta}) - \theta \rightarrow 0$  for $n\rightarrow\infty$ 
		- It's required for its variance to disappear asymptotically:
			- $Var(\widehat{\theta})\rightarrow 0$  for $n\rightarrow\infty$

**Accuracy and Precision**: ^df2f06
 - Accuracy is linked with bias and are inverse:
	- More accurate when less bias.
 - Precision is linked with variance and are inverse:
	- More precise when less variance.

Notation: 
 - $\mu$: population mean.
 - $\bar X$: sample mean, estimator of $\mu$.
 - $\bar x$: a particular estimate of $\mu$.
 - $\sigma$: population standard deviation.
 - $s$: sample standard deviation, estimator of $\sigma$.
 - $\sigma^{2}$: population variance.
 - $s^{2}$: sample variance, estimator of $\sigma$.
 - $\sigma(\widehat\theta)$: standard error of estimator $\widehat\theta$ (same as standard deviation).
 - $s(\widehat\theta)$ or $\widehat\sigma(\widehat\theta)$: estimated standard error.
