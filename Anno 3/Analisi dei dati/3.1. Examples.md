[[3. Intervals#^93452d||MLE-based confidence interval]]:
- $X_{1},\ldots, X_{n}$ simple random value from a normal distribution with mean 0 and unknown variance $\sigma^{2}$ and want to construct a 90% confidence interval:
	- Density: $f(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)^{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x}{\sigma}\right)^{2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}e^{-\left(\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)}$
	- Variance: $\hat{\sigma}^{2} = \frac{1}{n}\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}(X_{i}-\bar{X})^{2} = \frac{1}{n}\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}^{2}$ 
		- Here $\frac{1}{n}$ is used instead of $\frac{1}{n-1}$ since the mean is known to be 0 and the degree of freedom it's not restrained by it.
	- [[2. Estimation methods#^c1cff4||Fisher information]]: $\mathcal{I}(\sigma^{2}) = \frac{n}{2\sigma^{4}}$
		- $l(\sigma^{2}) = \ln(f(\sigma^{2})) = \ln((2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}})+\ln((\sigma^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}})+\ln\left(e^{-\left(\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)}\right)$ = 
			- $=-\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi) - \frac{1}{2}\ln(\sigma^{2}) - \left(\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)$ 
		- $l'(\sigma^{2}) = \frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma^{2}}l = -\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}} + \frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma^{4}}$
		- $l''(\sigma^{2}) = \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\sigma^{4}}l = \frac{1}{2\sigma^{4}} - \frac{x^{2}}{\sigma^{6}}$
		- $\mathcal{I}_{1}(\sigma^{2}) = \mathbb{E}\{-l''(\sigma^{2})\} = -\frac{1}{2\sigma^{4}} + \frac{\mathbb{E}[x^{2}]}{\sigma^{6}} = -\frac{1}{2\sigma^{4}} + \frac{\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{6}} = \frac{1}{2\sigma^{4}}$ 
			- $\mathbb{E}[x^{2}] = Var(x) = \sigma^{2}$
	- For large $n$: $\hat{\sigma}^{2}\approx N\left(\sigma^{2}, \frac{1}{\mathcal{I}(\sigma^{2})}\right) = N\left(\sigma^{2}, \frac{2\sigma^{4}}{n}\right)$    
	- Confidence interval: $\hat{\sigma}^{2}\pm z_{0.1/2} \mathcal{I}(\hat{\sigma}^{2})^{-1/2} = \hat{\sigma}^{2}\pm z_{0.05} \sqrt{\frac{2\hat{\sigma}^{4}}{n}} = \hat{\sigma}^{2}\pm z_{0.05} \hat{\sigma}^{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}$  
		- Standard values:
			- 90% confidence = $z_{0.05} = 1.645$
			- 95% confidence = $z_{0.025} = 1.96$
			- 99% confidence = $z_{0.005} = 2.576$
	- Suppose $n=60$ and $\sum\limits^{60}_{i=1}X_{i}^{2} = 164.83$
		- Confidence interval: $\frac{164.83}{60}\pm 1.64(\frac{164.83}{60})\sqrt{\frac{2}{60}} \approx 2.75\pm 0.82 \approx [1.93, 3.57]$
	- Standard deviation:
		- If we want the 90% confidence interval for the standard deviation $\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^{2}}$ a transformation is needed.
		- The square root (transformation) can be applied directly to the 90% interval: $[\sqrt{1.93},\sqrt{3.57}] = [1.39,1.89]$

[[3. Intervals#^7ef37b||Confidence intervals and transformations]] :
- The distribution of the operating time (in hours) without failure of a critical engine component has density: $f(\vartheta) = \left\{\begin{array}{lc} 0 & x<0 \\ \vartheta e^{-\vartheta x} & x\geq 0 \end{array}\right.$ 
	- Unknown $\vartheta$ but its $99\%$ interval is $[0.00001, 0.09]$ and our flight takes 8 hours.
	- Let $T$  be the operating time of the critical engine component.
	- Probability that the component does not fail before landing: $\mathbb{P}(T>8) = \int_{8}^{\infty} f(\vartheta)dx = \int_{8}^{\infty} \vartheta e^{-\vartheta x} dx = e^{-8\vartheta}$ 
	- Confidence interval of the transformation:
		- $e^{-8\vartheta}$ is monotone decreasing.
		- Interval: $[e^{-8(0.09)},e^{-8(0.00001)}] = [0.487, 0.999]$
			- This interval suggests we are 99% confident that the true reliability of the component over an 8-hour flight lies somewhere between about 48.7% and 99.9%.
			- This is a very wide interval with a lower endpoint is as likely to fail as a coin flip.
