**Glossary**: 
- **Population**: all units of interest.
- **Parameter**: numerical characteristic of the population of interest. Notation: $\theta$.
- **Census** (*censimento*): observation of the whole population to value $\theta$.
- **Sample**: subset/subgroup of the population used to evaluate/estimate $\theta$ when the census is not possible.
- **Statistic**: any function of the data (either by census or sample).
- **Estimator**: statistic used to reconstruct $\theta$. Notation: $\widehat\theta$.
- **Estimate** (*stima*): value of the estimator corresponding to the studied sample.

**Errors**: 
- The difference between the estimate (value of $\widehat{\theta}$) and the parameter ($\theta$) is due to two types of errors:
	- **Sampling** **errors**:
		- Inevitable.
		- Due to the limit of the sample.
		- Decreases more and more as the sample is bigger and if the estimator $\widehat\theta$ is reasonable.
	- **Non**-**sampling** **errors**:
		- Evitabile.
		- Due to the use of inappropriate sampling scheme, wrong statistical techniques or the use of an estimator $\widehat\theta$ that's not appropriated.
		- May or may not change with a bigger sample.

Random sampling:
- **Simple random sampling** is a sampling design where units are collected from the entire population independently of each other, all being equally likely to be sampled.
- It is a possible way to reduce non-sampling errors.
- Random sampling reduces distortions and having a specific subgroup of the population.
- Observations collected by means of a simple random sampling design are **IID** (independent and identically distributed).

Notation: 
 - $\mu$: population mean.
 - $\overline X$: sample mean, estimator of $\mu$.
 - $\overline x$: a particular estimate of $\mu$.
 - $\sigma$: population standard deviation.
 - $s$: sample standard deviation, estimator of $\sigma$.
 - $\sigma^{2}$: population variance.
 - $s^{2}$: sample variance, estimator of $\sigma$.
 - $\sigma(\widehat\theta)$: standard error of estimator $\widehat\theta$ (same as standard deviation).
 - $s(\widehat\theta)$ or $\widehat\sigma(\widehat\theta)$: estimated standard error.

Simple descriptive statistics:
- **Mean**: measuring the average value of sample. 
	- Is an **estimator** of the [[3. Variabili casuali#^b93ced |population mean]] ($\mu = \mathbb{E}(X)$)
	- Is the arithmetic average: $$\overline{X} = \frac{1}{n}\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1} X_{i} = \frac{X_{1}+...+X_{n}}{n}$$
	- Bias (distorsione): ^498016
		- Is defined as $Bias(\hat\theta) = \mathbb{E}(\hat\theta - \theta) = \mathbb{E}(\hat\theta) - \theta$ 
			- This equality is due to the fact that since $\theta$ is a constant, $\mathbb{E}(\theta) = \theta$ .
		- Unbiasedness means that collecting a large number of samples and computing them on $\hat\theta$, on the average we hit the unknown parameter $\theta$ exactly.
			- Unbiased estimator neither underestimate nor overestimate the parameter on the long run.
		- An estimator $\hat\theta$ is unbiased for a parameter $\theta$ if its expectation equals the parameter: $\mathbb{E}(\hat\theta) = \theta$ for all possible values of $\theta$.
		- Sample mean estimates $\mu$ unbiasedly because its expectation is: $\mathbb{E}(\overline X) = \mathbb{E}(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}) = \frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{i}) = \frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mu = \frac{n\mu}{n} =\mu$
	- Consistency:
		- An estimator $\hat\theta$ is consistent for a parameter $\theta$ if the probability of its sampling error converges to 0 as the sample size increases to infinity.
			- $\mathbb{P}\{|\hat\theta - \theta|>\epsilon\} \rightarrow 0 \;\mbox{as}\; n\rightarrow\infty \; \forall\; \epsilon > 0$
			- $|\widehat{\theta}-\theta|$ is called estimation error. 
		- The consistency of $\overline{X}$ follows directly from Chebyshev's inequality:
			- $Var(\overline{X}) = Var(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}) = \frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}Var(X_{i}) = \frac{n\sigma^2}{n^{2}} =\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n}$
			- Chebyshev's inequality: $\mathbb{P}\{|X - \mu|>\epsilon\}\leq \frac{Var(X)}{\epsilon^{2}} = \left(\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon}\right)^2$
				- Meaning a random variable X belongs to the interval $\mu \pm \epsilon$ with probability of at least $1-\left(\sigma/\epsilon\right)^2$
			- $\Rightarrow \mathbb{P}\{|\overline{X} - \mu|>\epsilon\}\leq \frac{\sigma^{2}\backslash n}{\epsilon^{2}} \rightarrow 0 \;as\; n\rightarrow\infty$
		- The consistency doesn't require $\widehat{\theta}$ to be unbiased:
			- It's required to be asymptotically unbiased:
				- $Bias(\widehat{\theta}) = \mathbb{E}(\widehat{\theta}) - \theta \rightarrow 0$  for $n\rightarrow\infty$ 
			- It's required for its variance to disappear asymptotically:
				- $Var(\widehat{\theta})\rightarrow 0$  for $n\rightarrow\infty$
	- Asymptotic normality:
		- By the **Central Limit Theorem**, the sample mean have approximately Standard Normal distribution if they computed from a large sample.
		- Central Limit Theorem: $Z=\frac{\overline{X}-\mathbf{E}(\overline{X})}{Std\overline{X}}=\frac{\sqrt{n}(\overline{X}-\mu)}{\sigma}\rightarrow N(0,1)\; as\; n\rightarrow\infty$
			- This is the same as $Z = \sqrt{n}(\overline{X}-\mu)\rightarrow N(0,\sigma^{2})$
- **Median**: measuring the central value.
	- Is the number that's exceeded by at most half of the observations and is preceded by at most a half of the observations.
		- $\left\{ \begin{array}{lcc} P\{X>M\} & \leq & 0.5 \\ P\{X<M\} & \geq & 0.5 \end{array}\right.$ 
	- Median il less prone to distortion from limit cases (less sensitive) than the mean.
		- Comparing mean and media:
			- $\left\{\begin{array}{lcc} \mbox{Symmetrical distribution} & \Rightarrow & M =\mu \\ \mbox{Right-skewed distribution} & \Rightarrow & M <\mu \\ \mbox{Left-skewed distribution} & \Rightarrow & M >\mu \end{array}\right.$
	- Computation of a population median:
		- This is done on the [[3. Variabili casuali#^20d770 |cumulative distribution function]].
		- Discrete distribution:
			- The equation $F(x)$ has either an interval of roots or none, so the median can either be:
				- Any number in the interval of the roots.
				- The smallest $x$ with $F(x) \geq 0.5$.
			- ![[Pasted image 20251127222528.png]]
		- Continuous distribution:
			- The equation $F(x) = 0.5$ needs to be solved to find the median.
		- Sample median:
			- A sample is always discrete, it consist of a finite number of observations.
				- Computing it will always be the same of a discrete distribution.
			- Cases:
				- Size sample $n$ is odd:
					- The $\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)$ -th smallest observation is the median.
				- Size sample $n$ is even:
					- Any number between $\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)$ -th smallest and $\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)$ -th smallest observations is the median.
- **Variance and standard deviation**: variability among observations.
	- The **sample variance** is an **estimator** of the [[3. Variabili casuali#^b32abc|population variance]] ($\sigma^{2}=Var(X)$):
		- $$s^{2}=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}(X_{i}-\widehat{X})^{2} = \frac{\sum\limits^{n}_{i=1}(X_{i}^{2})-n\widehat{X}^{2}}{n-1}$$
		- $(X_{i}-\widehat{X})$ are called deviation of the mean. 
		- $(\frac{1}{n-1})$ is used to ensure that $s^{2}$ is unbiased ($Bias(s^{2})=\mathbb{E}(s^{2})-\sigma^{2}=0$).
	- The **sample standard deviation** is the square root of the sample variance:
		- $s = \sqrt{s^{2}}$
		- It measures variability in the same units as $X$ and is an **estimator** of the standard deviation ($\sigma$).
		- The sample standard deviation is [[1. Population, sample and statistics#^498016|biased]] because the square root is non linear ($\mathbb{E}(\sqrt{s^{2}})\neq \sqrt{\mathbb{E}(s^{2})}$):
			- $Bias(s) = \mathbb{E}(s)-\sigma\neq 0$
	- Sample variance and standard deviation are:
		- **Consistent**:
			- $s^{2}\rightarrow \sigma^{2}$ when $n\rightarrow\infty$
			- $s\rightarrow \sigma$ when $n\rightarrow\infty$
		- **Asymptotically normal**:
			- $s^{2}\rightarrow N(\sigma^{2}, Var(s^{2}))$ when $n\rightarrow\infty$
			- $s\rightarrow N(\sigma, \frac{1}{4\sigma^{2}}Var(s^{2}))$ when $n\rightarrow\infty$
- **Standard Error**: 
	- Is the standard deviation of an estimator $\widehat{\theta}$.
	- This shows reliability and precision of estimators.
- **Mean squared error**:
	- Accuracy is linked with bias and are inverse:
		- More accurate when less bias.
	- Precision is linked with variance and are inverse:
		- More precise when less variance.
	- Mean squared error (**MSE**) measures the estimator quality combining variance and biasness:
		- $MSE(\widehat\theta) = \mathbb{E}(\widehat\theta-\theta)^{2} = Var(\widehat\theta)+Bias(\widehat\theta)^{2}$
- **Interquartile Range**:
	- Is the difference between the first and third quartile:
		- $IQR = Q_{3}-Q_{1}$
	- The interquartile range is estimated by the **sample interquartile range**:
		- $\widehat{IQR} = \widehat{Q}_{3}-\widehat{Q}_{1}$
	- It measures the variability of the data and it is used to detect possible outliers.
		- A rule of thumb is the rule of **1.5(IQR)**:
			- $IQR$ is calculated.
			- $\widehat{Q}_{1} - 1.5(\widehat{IQR})$ is calculated.
			- $\widehat{Q}_{3} + 1.5(\widehat{IQR})$ is calculated.
			- Samples outside of the interval $[\widehat{Q}_{1} - 1.5(\widehat{IQR}), \widehat{Q}_{3} + 1.5(\widehat{IQR})]$ might be  an outlier.
