**Hypothesis**:
 - Hypothesis is using sample data to assess competing claims.
 - System of hypothesis: ^6854a8
	 - We compare two mutually exclusive hypothesis:
		 - The null hypothesis $H_{0}$:
			 - Is the "*status quo*", what is already believed until clear evidence against it is provided by the data.
		 - The alternative hypothesis $H_{A}$:
			 - The alternative hypothesis have different forms:
				 - Two-sided alternative (two-tailed test):
					 - $\begin{array}{lcr}H_{0}:\theta = \theta_{0} && \mbox{vs} && H_{A}:\theta \neq \theta_{0}\end{array}$ 
				 - Left-sided alternative (one-tailed test):
				     - $\begin{array}{lcr}H_{0}:\theta \geq \theta_{0} && \mbox{vs} && H_{A}:\theta < \theta_{0}\end{array}$ 
				 - Right-sided alternative (one-tailed test):
				     - $\begin{array}{lcr}H_{0}:\theta \leq \theta_{0} && \mbox{vs} && H_{A}:\theta > \theta_{0}\end{array}$ 
			 - In one-sided test we can use $H_{0}:\theta = \theta_{0}$ since if we reject it we also reject $\begin{array}{lcr}H_{0}:\theta \geq \theta_{0} && \mbox{or} && H_{0}:\theta \leq \theta_{0}\end{array}$
	 - **Test** is the sampling experiment used to evaluate the hypotheses and leads to two possible decisions:
		 - Fail to reject $H_{0}$ due to lack of evidence against it from the data.
		 - Reject $H_{0}$ due to sufficient evidence against it from the data.
	 - Presumption of innocence:
		 - In trials, the defendant is considered innocent unless sufficient strong evidence is presente.
		 - The same principle apply here, the $H_{0}$ persist until the data provides enough evidence against it.
			 - The $H_{0}$ holds a privileged role being maintained until proven otherwise.
	 - Errors:
		 - Decisions are based on sample data and conclusions can be affected by sampling errors.
		 - The two possible errors are:
			 - Type I error:
				 - Rejecting $H_{0}$ when is true.
				 - More serious (analogous to convicting an innocent person).
			 - Type II error:
				 - Not rejecting $H_{0}$ when is false.
		 - We can construct procedures to minimize errors:
			 - First we set a maximum acceptable value for the probability of the type I error (hard limit since is more serious).
				 - **Significance level** is the probability of a type I error: $\alpha = \mathbb{P}(\mbox{reject } H_{0} \mbox{ when it is true})$
				 - Steps:
					 - Identify a test statistics (a function of the data used to discriminate between $H_{0}$ and $H_{A}$)
						 - Usually a transformation of an estimator of $\hat{\theta}$ with a known distribution $f_{0}$ under $H_{o}$.
					 - Determine the values of the test statistics for accepting or rejecting $H_{0}$ at the fixed significance level $\alpha$.
						 - There are called **acceptance region** $\mathcal{A}$ and **rejection region** $\mathcal{R}$. ^de0ef7
						 - ![[Pasted image 20260619105344.png]]
					 - Make a decision based on the observed value's presence in the acceptance o rejection regions and interpret the result.
			 - We then choose a procedure that minimizes the probability of the type II error (less strict since is less serious).
				 - **Power** is the probability of not making a type II error (rejecting $H_{0}$ when $H_{A}$ is false): $\beta(\theta) = \mathbb{P}(\mbox{reject } H_{0} \mbox{ when } H_{A} \mbox{ is true})$
				 - We need to maximaze the power.
 - Z-test (test statistic): ^34cf0f
	 - Z-statistic:
		 - Suppose $\hat{\theta}$ is unbiased and asymptotically normal.
		 - Null hypothesis $H_{0}:\theta = \theta_{0}$, a natural statistic is $Z = \frac{\hat{\theta}-\theta_{0}}{SE(\hat{\theta})}  = \frac{\hat{\theta}-\theta_{0}}{\sqrt{Var(\hat{\theta})}}$
			 - [[4. Hypothesis testing#^0af9cf||Sample]] with $\mu=\mu_{0}$ with estimator $\bar{X}$, The Z-statistic is: $Z = \frac{\bar{X}-\mu_{0}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}$
		 - Values of Z:
			 - Close to 0 means insufficient evidence to reject $H_{0}$
			 - Far from 0 means evidence against $H_{0}$
	 - Right-sided statistic:
		 - Alternative hypothesis: $\begin{array}{lcr}H_{0}:\theta = \theta_{0} && \mbox{vs} && H_{A}:\theta > \theta_{0}\end{array}$
		 - Reject "too large" values of Z statistic: $\begin{array}{lcr}\mathcal{R} = [z_{\alpha},+\infty) && \mbox{and} && \mathcal{A} = (-\infty,z_{\alpha})\end{array}$
		 - Significance level: $\mathbb{P}(Z\in\mathcal{R}|H_{0}) = \mathbb{P}(Z>z_{\alpha}) = \alpha$
	 - Left-sided statistic:
		 - Alternative hypothesis: $\begin{array}{lcr}H_{0}:\theta = \theta_{0} && \mbox{vs} && H_{A}:\theta < \theta_{0}\end{array}$
		 - Reject "too small" values of Z statistic: $\begin{array}{lcr}\mathcal{R} = (-\infty,-z_{\alpha}] && \mbox{and} && \mathcal{A} = (-z_{\alpha},+\infty)\end{array}$
		 - Significance level: $\mathbb{P}(Z\in\mathcal{R}|H_{0}) = \mathbb{P}(Z<-z_{\alpha}) = \alpha$
	 - Two-sided statistics:
		 - Alternative hypothesis: $\begin{array}{lcr}H_{0}:\theta = \theta_{0} && \mbox{vs} && H_{A}:\theta \neq \theta_{0}\end{array}$
		 - Reject both "too small" and "too large" values of Z statistic: $\begin{array}{lcr}\mathcal{R} = (-\infty,-z_{\alpha/2}]\cup [z_{\alpha/2},+\infty) && \mbox{and} && \mathcal{A} = (-z_{\alpha/2},z_{\alpha/2})\end{array}$
		 - Significance level: $\mathbb{P}(Z\in\mathcal{R}|H_{0}) = \mathbb{P}(Z<-z_{\alpha/2})+\mathbb{P}(Z>z_{\alpha/2}) = \frac{\alpha}{2}+ \frac{\alpha}{2} = \alpha$
		 - **Duality** Theorem: ^7387a8
			 - A Z test for two sided alternatives does not reject the null hypothesis at significance level $\alpha$ **if and only if** the confidence interval for $\theta$ based on the Z statistics with confidence level $1-\alpha$ contains $\theta_{0}$
			 - Proof:
				 - The null hypothesis is not rejected if and only if $-z_{\alpha/2} < \frac{\hat{\theta}-\theta_{0}}{SE(\hat{\theta})} < z_{\alpha/2} \Rightarrow \left|\frac{\hat{\theta}-\theta_{0}}{SE(\hat{\theta})}\right|<z_{\alpha/2} \Rightarrow |\hat{\theta}-\theta_{0}|<z_{\alpha/2}SE(\hat{\theta})$

 - T-test:
	 - Small samples:
		 - For small $n$ the Z statistics may differ substantially from the normal distribution and type I errors can occur if using it.
	 - One-sample *t*-test:
		 - To evaluate a the hypothesis of a small sample from a normal distribution (with unknown mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^{2}$) we use the [[3. Intervals#^2eeea8||t statistics]]: $T = \frac{\sqrt{n}(\bar{X}-\mu_{0})}{S}$
			 - We are interested in testing $H_{0}:\mu = \mu_{0}$ against the alternatives:
				 - $H_{A}:\theta \neq \theta_{0}$
				 - $H_{A}:\theta < \theta_{0}$
				 - $H_{A}:\theta > \theta_{0}$
		 - One-tailed test:
			 - Right sided:
				 - Alternative hypothesis: $\begin{array}{lcr}H_{0}:\theta = \theta_{0} && \mbox{vs} && H_{A}:\theta > \theta_{0}\end{array}$
				 - Reject "too large" values of T statistic: $\begin{array}{lcr}\mathcal{R} = [t_{\alpha},+\infty) && \mbox{and} && \mathcal{A} = (-\infty,t_{\alpha})\end{array}$
				 - Significance level: $\mathbb{P}(T\in\mathcal{R}|H_{0}) = \mathbb{P}(T>t_{\alpha}) = \alpha$
			 - Left sided:
				 - Alternative hypothesis: $\begin{array}{lcr}H_{0}:\theta = \theta_{0} && \mbox{vs} && H_{A}:\theta < \theta_{0}\end{array}$
				 - Reject "too small" values of T statistic: $\begin{array}{lcr}\mathcal{R} = (-\infty,-t_{\alpha}] && \mbox{and} && \mathcal{A} = (-t_{\alpha},+\infty)\end{array}$
				 - Significance level: $\mathbb{P}(T\in\mathcal{R}|H_{0}) = \mathbb{P}(T<-t_{\alpha}) = \alpha$
		 - Two-tailed test:
			 - Alternative hypothesis: $\begin{array}{lcr}H_{0}:\theta = \theta_{0} && \mbox{vs} && H_{A}:\theta \neq \theta_{0}\end{array}$
			 - Reject both "too small" and "too large" values of Z statistic: $\begin{array}{lcr}\mathcal{R} = (-\infty,-t_{\alpha/2}]\cup [t_{\alpha/2},+\infty) && \mbox{and} && \mathcal{A} = (-t_{\alpha/2},t_{\alpha/2})\end{array}$
			 - Significance level: $\mathbb{P}(T\in\mathcal{R}|H_{0}) = \mathbb{P}(T<-t_{\alpha/2})+\mathbb{P}(T>t_{\alpha/2}) = \frac{\alpha}{2}+ \frac{\alpha}{2} = \alpha$
	 - Two-sample *t*-test:
		 - Null hypothesis: $H_{0}:\mu_{X}-\mu_{Y}=D$
		 - Alternate hypothesis:
			 - $H_{0}:\mu_{X}-\mu_{Y}\neq D$
			 - $H_{0}:\mu_{X}-\mu_{Y}>D$
			 - $H_{0}:\mu_{X}-\mu_{Y}<D$
		 - It is possible sometimes to set $D=0$.

 - P-value: ^4606cf
	 - P-value also called Observed Significance Level avoids fixing a significance level in advance and instead measures the strength of the evidence against the null hypothesis.
		 - When the null hypothesis is rejected, the test is said to be significant.
	 - Definition:
		 - The probability of obtaining a value of the test statistic equal to or more extreme than the observed one, computed under the assumption that $H_{0}$ is true.
	 - Alternative definition:
		 - The smallest significance level at which we would reject the null hypothesis.
		 - The largest significance level at which we would **fail** to reject the null hypothesis.
			 - These definitions are equivalent.
	 - Cases:
		 - If the p-value is “large”, the observed result is not particularly extreme and we cannot reject $H_{0}$ 
		 - If the p-value is “small”, the observed result is indeed extreme and we reject $H_{0}$
	 - Computation:
		 - Right sided:
			 - $p = \mathbb{P}(Z\geq z) = 1-\Phi(z)$
		 - Left sided:
			 - $p = \mathbb{P}(Z\leq z) = \Phi(z)$
		 - Two sided:
			 - $p = \mathbb{P}(|Z|>|z|) = \mathbb{P}(Z>|z|)+\mathbb{P}(Z< -|z|) =$
			 - $= 2\mathbb{P}(Z>|z|) = 2\{1-\mathbb{P}(Z<|z|)\} = 2\{1-\Phi(z)\}$

 - Dependent means:
	 - Treating dependent data as independent and using Z or t statistics can lead to higher rates of type I and II errors.
	 - Paired data:
		 - $(X_{1},Y_{1}),\ldots,(X_{n},Y_{n})$
			 - We have paired data when the variables X and Y are observed on the same statistical units.
		 - Test formulation:
			 - Null hypothesis: $H_{0}:\mu_{X}=\mu_{Y}$
			 - Test statistic: $Z = \frac{\bar{X}-\bar{Y}}{\sqrt{\frac{S^{2}_{X}}{n}+\frac{S^{2}_{Y}}{n}-\frac{2S_{XY}}{n}}}$
				 - $S_{XY}$ is the covariance between $X$ and $Y$.
		 - Difference approach:
			 - It is simpler to consider the differences: $D_{i}=X_{i}-Y_{i}$
			 - Null hypothesis: $H_{0}:\mu_{D}=0$
			 - Test statistics: $Z = \sqrt{n} \frac{\bar{D}}{S_{D}}$

Personal explanation:
 - Significance level $\alpha$ and $z_\alpha$ ​are inversely proportional (smaller $\alpha$ gives larger $z_{\alpha}$). The Z-statistic is the standardized position of our observed data on the normal distribution under $H_{0}$. 
 - For a one-sided test, $z_{\alpha}$ ​marks a single cut-off: if $Z$ falls beyond it, we reject $H_{0}$ in favour of $H_{A}$. For a two-sided test, the cut-offs are $\pm z_{\alpha/2}$. 
 - Landing outside this region doesn't "prove" $H_{A}$, ​it means the evidence is strong enough to reject $H_{0}$ at that significance level. Landing inside doesn't "prove" $H_{0}$, it just means we don't have enough evidence to reject it. 
 - The larger $z_{\alpha}$ is (i.e. the smaller $\alpha$ is), the stricter the standard, and the harder it becomes to reject $H_{0}$ ​.
