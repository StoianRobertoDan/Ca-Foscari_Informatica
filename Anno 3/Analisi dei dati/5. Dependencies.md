**Variable dependence**:
 - Terminology:
	 - $X$: *predictor* variable.
	 - $Y$: *response* variable.
	 - Conditional expectation: $G(x) = E(Y|X=x)$
	  - $\beta_{0}$: intercept.
		   - Value predicted by the model when $x=0$: $\beta_{0} = G(0)$
		   - Often it does not have a meaningful interpretation.
	  - $\beta_{1}$: slope.
		   - The change in the regression function when the predictor increases by one unit: $\beta_{1} = G(x+1)-G(x) = \{\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}(x-1)\}-\{\beta_{0}-\beta_{1}x\}$
		   - Tells how much the *response* variable changes when the *predictor* variable increases.
 - Regression models:
	 - **Simple linear regression** (*regression line*):
		 - $G(x) = \beta_{0}+\beta_{1}x$
	 - Multiple linear regression:
		 - $G(x) = \beta_{0}+\beta_{1}x+\ldots+\beta_{n}x_{n}$
 - Least squares method:
	 - Data consists of $n$ pairs of observations: $(y_{1},x_{1}),\ldots,(y_{n},x_{n})$
	 - Estimate $\hat{G}(x)$ of the regression function $G(x)$ computed by minimizing the sum of squared differences between the observed responses and the corresponding predictions based on the regression model.
		 - $\hat{y_{1}} = \hat{G}(x_{1}),\ldots,\hat{y_{n}} = \hat{G}(x_{n})$
		 - Residuals: $e_{i}=y_{i}-\hat{y}_{i}$ 
		 - Sum of squared residuals: $\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}^{2} = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}-\hat{y}_{i})^{2}$
		 - Minimizing: $Q(\beta_{0},\beta_{1}) = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}-\hat{y}_{i})^{2} = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}-(\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}x_i))^{2}$ 
			 - Solving: $\frac{\partial Q(\beta_{0},\beta_{1})}{\partial\beta_{0}}=0$ and $\frac{\partial Q(\beta_{0},\beta_{1})}{\partial\beta_{1}}=0$
			 - Solution: $\hat{\beta}_{0} = \bar{y}-\hat{\beta}_{1}\bar{x}$ and $\hat{\beta}_{1} = \frac{S_{xy}}{S^{2}_{x}}$
				 - $S_{xy} = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-\bar{x})(y_{i}-\bar{y})$
				 - $S^{2}_{x} =  \frac{1}{n-1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-\bar{x})^{2}$
 - Regression and correlation:
	 - The estimator of the slope can be expressed by the sample correlation coefficient: $r_{xy} = \frac{S_{xy}}{S_{x}S_{y}}$
		 - $\hat{\beta}_{1}>0$ if $X$ and $Y$ are positively correlated.
		 - $\hat{\beta}_{1}<0$ if $X$ and $Y$ are negatively correlated.
		 - $\hat{\beta}_{1}=0$ if $X$ and $Y$ are not correlated.
	 - The total variation is described by the total sum of squares: $SS_{TOT} = TSS = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}-\bar{y}_{i})^{2} = (n-1)s^{2}_{y}$
		 - $SS_{TOT} = SS_{REG}+SS_{ERR}$
			 - Explained sum of squares $SS_{REG} = ESS = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\hat{y}_{i}-\bar{y}_{i})^{2}$
			 - Residual error sum of squares $SS_{ERR} = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}-\hat{y}_{i})^{2}$
		 - ![[Pasted image 20260711215539.png]]
		 - Coefficient of determination: $R^{2} = \frac{SS_{REG}}{SS_{TOT}} = 1- \frac{SS_{ERR}}{SS_{TOT}}$
			 - Takes values between 0 and 1.
			 - The larger $R^{2}$, the better the fit of the regression model to the data.
 - Spurious correlation:
	 - Relation between two variables is caused by a third variable to which the first two are related.
 - Confidence intervals and hypothesis testing:
	 - Simple linear regression model with normal errors: $Y_{i} = \beta_{0}+\beta_{1}x+\epsilon_{i}$
	 - To construct confidence intervals and perform hypothesis tests for $\beta_{1}$, we need to introduce additional assumptions:
		 - The errors are independent normal random variables with mean zero and variance $\sigma^{2}$
		 - The predictor $X$ is a non-random quantity.
	 - Under this assumptions $\hat{\beta}_{1}$ is an unbiased estimator of $\beta_{1}$ and has an exact normal distribution.
	 - Since $\hat{\beta}_{1}$ is normally distributed we can use the *t* statistic to construct the interval for $\beta_{1}$:
		 - $(1-\alpha)$ confidence interval: $\hat{\beta}_{1} \pm t_{\alpha/2}\hat{SE}(\hat{\beta}_{1})$
			 - $t_{\alpha/2}$ is the $(1-\alpha/2)$ quantile of the Student's *t* distribution with $n-2$ degree of freedom.
	 - Hypothesis testing:
		 - Null hypothesis: $H_{0}:\beta_{1}=\beta_{1}^{0}$
		 - T-statistic: $T = \frac{\hat{\beta}_{1}-\beta_{1}^{0}}{\hat{SE}(\hat{\beta}_{1})}$
			 - T follows a Student's *t* distribution with $n-2$ degree of freedom.
		 - If the significance test indicates that we fail to reject the null hypothesis, this means that there is no linear relationship between the predictor and the response.
			 - A non linear relationship may still be present.
